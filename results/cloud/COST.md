## Measured work

The first 3 tuning days (83,861 positions from 213 vessels; 84,699 arrivals with the simulated feed's duplicates), run four ways. CPU time per position:

| Path | CPU per position | Against batch |
|---|---|---|
| Batch: detectors once over the span | 0.0142 ms | 1× |
| Stream fed hourly, in memory | 0.177 ms | 12× |
| Service, 10-minute ticks with checkpoint and sink I/O | 1.42 ms | 99× |
| One position at a time, in memory (mean) | 10.9 ms | 768× |

Lower latency costs more compute: each push that moves the watermark runs every detector over the vessels' tails, whatever it carries. Of the 300 positions pushed alone after a 24-hour warm-up, 27 moved it (median 0.20 ms, 95th percentile 120 ms).

Service ticks: 434 ticks, 95th percentile 0.55 s; 8.8 objects (0.69 MB) written per tick; checkpoint mean 1.00 MB (largest 2.13 MB); loading it back takes 30 ms from 36 files. One position is 203 bytes as JSON.

### Does one process keep up?

| Volume | Service vCPUs busy | Slow Lambda tick | Checkpoint | Lambda keeps up | Fargate keeps up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regional, median day | 0.01 | 9 s | 15 MB | yes | yes |
| Regional, busiest day | 0.01 | 14 s | 25 MB | yes | yes |
| Global, GFW 2016 | 0.33 | 410 s | 708 MB | yes | yes |
| Global, GFW later | 1.80 | 2,253 s | 3,895 MB | no | no |

vCPUs busy is the average share of one vCPU the ticks need. A Lambda tick must end within its 600 s window and Lambda's 900 s limit; the slow tick is the measured 95th percentile, checkpoint load included, scaled with traffic. The Fargate task needs vCPUs busy below 1 and its 2 GB to hold at least the checkpoint. Where neither keeps up, the stream would have to be split between processes by vessel, with encounters exchanged between them. That is not built, so those rows price compute the design does not yet have.

## Assumptions and limits

- Work is scaled from the extract in proportion to positions (provisional). The extract holds one group's fishing vessels only (7% of the box's rows); a tick's work follows the state the stream carries, assumed to grow with traffic.
- Laptop CPU seconds stand for Arm vCPU seconds (provisional); Lambda gives one vCPU at 1,769 MB, so CPU-bound work is priced at that size.
- The per-message design's compute is this stream fed one row at a time, in memory: a lower bound, since a stateless function would also load each vessel's state. Its DynamoDB item size (1 KB) is assumed (provisional).
- Regional volumes are MarineCadastre's published rows, already filtered to one a minute; a raw receiver feed is larger.
- List prices before free tiers, one region, no data transfer, logs or alert delivery.

## The same design on Google Cloud

The service maps onto Apache Beam with little change. The spool becomes a Pub/Sub subscription; the reorder hold and watermark become Beam's event-time watermark with allowed lateness; the per-vessel tails, segmenter and episode lifecycle become a stateful DoFn keyed by vessel, with timers settling episodes; the checkpoint is Dataflow's own state, so the store disappears; encounters re-key resampled positions by H3 cell, as in GFW's pipelines, which run on Beam and BigQuery; and the alert sink becomes a BigQuery table. No GCP prices were researched, so this note does not price it.
